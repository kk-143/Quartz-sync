# what they eat in Belgium
There are many regions in Belgium but and food culture varied greatly between them. Most notably, the Northern (Flander) would have very different meals compare to the Southern (Wallonie). This is from my observation only, you take it with tons of salts

Typically, people have 3 meals
- Dejeuner (breakfast), before 9 am, at home with sliced bread and somethings
- Le repas de midi (lunch), from 12 am - 2 pm, eating at their office, a sandwich or something similar like salad
- Tea time: Do they have gouter or Kaffee und Kuchen time ? i don't think so, but they do have coffee break if they need it. 
- Diner/souper(dinner) after 6 pm, eating at home, biggest meal of the day. Something hearty like soup or stew, with more bread or pasta.


# Meal planning
## Money
First is budget, you should plan for how much you spend on food first hand to deter overbuying which very easily done when living alone. 100 eu per month is already on the crazy side, around 200 - 300 is the normal range so everyday you can eat up to 10 eu. Eating out is very expensive, a sandwich or snack is 5 eu, a durum (kebab in belgium) is 13 eu, a drink in starbuck is around 9 eu. In short you cannot eat out with a 300 food budget, eating out is on "another" budget altogether (exploration, travel, entertainment). On the other hand, cooking for yourself is much cheaper, but it would still cost much more than living in Vietnam, but aim for the 250 eu/month of grocery, that is the first step. Please don't try living with 100 eu/month, it's close to torture.

## Protein
There are 3 types of protein in this section: vegan, common and strange protein. The vegan option won't have dairy product, they are in a different category. This section is the most expensive (for most people), so careful of your budget here.

Aim for at least 30 grams of protein per day or 150 gram (raw) of common protein. UCLA recommend around 
> Amount needed = (0.8 - 1.6) * Your weight (kg)

But protein is in your carb, in your snack and in your drinks, too. So the least amount you should aim for in this section is **30 grams or 150 grams of raw meat**.

## Fat
Fat is a very controversial topic. It depends a whole lot about your belief and your cooking preference. In Belgium you can buy many types of fat that cover most common usages, the listing below will cover from most suitable to Vietnamese.

- Seed oil:
	- The cheapest, from 2 eu /L. Sell in 1L can and 5L can
	- Canola oil (Huile de colza) is the most common oil, followed by Sunflower oil (Huile de tournesol - btw tournesol means turn (tourne) to the sun (sol)).
	- Neutral oil, can be use similar to  vegetable oils in Vietnam. Buy 1L asap
- Flavor oil
	- For example: sesame oil, chilly oil...
	- Often can only be found in asian store => not many choices.
	- Pricey, from 3 - 5 euro/bottle of 200 - 400 mL
	- Can be bought on amazon if required.
	- Mostly for Korean, Chinese or Japanese foods. Buy some if you need but remember that most likely you can't finish a bottle in 3 months
- Olive oil
	- A little expensive: 
		- Cheapest one from 5 euro/bottle of 250 ml. 
		- Best one in store could get to 20 euro/ bottle of 500 mL
	- Often have better quality and taste than the same product in Vietnam
	- Must have for Italian foods. Buy asap if you eats salad or Italians. Just buy the cheap one from the local brand, the quality has already surpass Vietnamese one.
	- Bio (organic) product could be found in bio store, very expensive. You could even travel to Spain, Italia or Greek for better one. 
- Butter
	- Most common fat in belgian cuisine
	- Often sell in bar of 250 gram. A normal portion is 10 - 20 grams, so a bar is about 30 meals.
	- Butter is required in almost every recipe here and cannot be replaced by seed oil, while seed oil could technically be replaced by butter. However, butter is too expensive for frying, but you can try.
	- Butter can be eat raw by spreading on bread or toast, sauteing. searing, thickening sauce by elmusify. it lubricate any dry food to become something edible, while using hot water only make it a miserable meal, using butter gives them life. Browning butter to give nutty flavor, infuse butter to carry flavors, etc..
	- I hate vegetable butter (magarine), so I only talking about butter from cow milk. You can check the percent of milk fat in the ingredient list, often it's from 60 - >80%. the others are water, salt, mineral... In general higher fat = more money worth but sometime, water contents is also good, for example, it will make the butter easier to spread, easier to melt into the batter or sauce as well as lighter when you eat it directly.
	- There is also fermented butter, it's tangy, have special flavor profile, a bit more expensive and need time to adapt to the taste, but it should be tried.
	- It's not as cheap as expected, commonly about 2.5 euro/bar of 250 gram. Expensive one are about 5 euro/bar
	- There are a lot of promos on butter, because it's essential. But like eggs, it will sold off very quickly, so grab it while you can. 
- Coconut oil
	- It's a vegan oil.
	- Only for baking.
	- If you trained your stomach in Vietnam for consuming this fat, then welcome but do not try it haphazardly here, it could wreck your guts.
- Other animal fat
	- Pig lard are easy to find, but you have to render them yourself. It's a half day project and your room would smell like a rotisserie. 
	- Cow fat or tallow is harder to find. It's often sold as oil for frittur. But fries oil often only sold in 10 L can.
	- Duck fat can rarely be found in market. 
	- You can also talk with your local butcher, in here they even have offal fat (organ fat and caul fat), which is super rare, expensive and require preorder, but if you can, it is the delicacy. Most famous recipe might be the Foie gras (goose liver). 
	- Rendered fat from high fat cuts is also a viable source of fat. Most common from cooking lard (or lardon), chicken skin or salmon. In those case, additional fat might not needed.
