This guide is only for Winter clothes. 
Winter in Belgium is from November to April. The temperature is hovering around 5 - 12 oC and 1 - 5 oC at night. It rarely snow, only ice formed on the roads, on the bridges and vegetations. However, it's dry and windy and sometimes, it can rains, heavily. Thus it's not dry in a dry way, it's the kind of dry from the dampness, the humid, chilly air somehow take away your heat very efficiently. When the sky is not so cloudy you can see the sun, but he will only shine from 9 am to 5 pm, like a normal, well respected working man. And the heat of the sun bring another layer of complexity in the temperate here. 

So the clothes is needed to fight against this weather, to protect us from 
- The chilly wind: it is the most dangerous, it attacks directly and take the body heat.
- The dampness: it's the 2nd one, always accumulating. Getting wet will cause the heat to lose 25 times faster. Keep yourself dry always.
- The dryness: The dry air will bleed your nose, chap your lips, dry your eyes and cracking on your finger. It won't kill you but it will make you miserable.
- The sun: He's a jerk, you thought that he would warm you up but instead he just make your clothing setup become miserable. He's so hot which make you sweat and stuffy and  easier to get a cold from a bad wind. 

What is our bad cases scenarios ?
- Indoor: you are so hot but you cannot take off a layer because you will be too cold. Your feet is freezing in your shoes and your arms are hard to move because of all your clothes. 
- Outdoor: 
	- It's so cold that you can die from hypothermia.
	- It's so hot that you feel like you are a living sauna.
		- It's raining 
	- It's so tired that you cannot move anymore, you just want to rest

I propose a system with 4 layers: Undie (1) Base (2) Thermal (3) Shell (4)
- Underwear:
	- Aims: Protect the base layer from your body fluids, change daily or more if needed.
	- How it works: it protect the base layer by absorbing the fluid
	- Should be synthetic (not cotton)
	- in 1 colors for easier to clean, I like black but yellow orange or red all fine
	- How many: 5 - 7 píeces
	- Thrown away when it made you feel stuffy.
- Base layer:
	- Aims: Keep you warm by reduce sweating
	- How it works: it wicks away (mao dẫn) the humid, transfer your sweat away from your body so that it stay dry and warm.
	- Synthetic
	- Buy it the same color as your underwear.
	- How many: 2 long sleeves and 2 legging
	- Don't tumble dry it, it will be shrink
- Middle layer:
	- Aims: keeps you warm 
	- 2 middle layers system:
		- Middle A: 1 sweater + 1 jacket for warm
			- The sweater should be made from soft wools like cashmere or merino
			- Jacket could be made from anything cotton or synthetic, you named it. But it will follow the normal rules of fabric
				- Natural fabric like cotton will maximize your breathability, the wicked moisture from your base ? Gone ! However, it will be sticky when it's rain, it will be soaked, or when coffee, tea or wine get on your jacket ? it's there forever
				- Synthetic fabric is cheap and wind resisted, this is the lightest setup. you would feel like autumn :) But, it's bad for the environment, you jacket is most likely 100% polyester, you are shedding microplastic everywhere you go...
			- Advantages: Best for fashion, soft sweater are very easy to pair with everything, the jacket gave you like every fashion choices, you will never feel like a potatoes in fabric oven with this setup.
			- Disadvantages:
				- You NEEDs to care for the sweater, the cheapest cashmere you can get is about 70 - 100 eu and it require cares: it cannot be machine wash, never hot water, no detergent, never wring it, never hang dry and I mean it, never even hang it when it dried in your clothes wardrobe, comb the lint. Wash it every 7 - 10 wears (once per week). Some stupid twat wash their merino shirt on machine with his jeans, and asked why his 100eu T shirt got holes in 2 months. Don't be like him. 
				- You will spend a lot of money on the Jacket too, it's fabulous anyway.
		- Middle B: 1 long sleeves + 1 sweater
			- The sleeves is made from cotton which often in your closet since autumn
			- The Sweater won't touch your body directly so it can be knitted and made from rougher material thus warmer and windproof.
			- Advantages: 
				- The sweater will be much cheaper, much easier to use. If you buy a synthetic one, it could be 5 times cheaper about 10 eu/one. Most sweater you can find in the normal shop are this kind. You could also buy an expensive one from rough wool and it will last for a life time. you also don't need (advised) to clean it often. It's a fisherman's sweater in the legend that can handle the north sea. 
				- The sleeves can be used as you pajama
			- Disadvantages:
				- It's rough, not pretty. You know the grandma's knitted sweater for Christmas ? It's this type, you don't need another jacket outside of this setup, it will only make you stuffy and even more ridiculous
				- It's not comfortable when you started to sweat. The sleeves would get soak and it will make you cold. this is why your jeans is so cold, jean is made from cotton. 
- Shell
	- Aims: Windproof and waterproof.
		- Also, can you use it anywhere else ? Especially in Vietnam ?
			- Don't buy an expensive piece without the chances to use them !!
	- How it works: Shell is the most outer layer of your setup, and despite most ppl recommend to you some kind of heavy hard shell that can save you in mount Alps. I don't think that's a good choice, for most of the time you are in Belgium. If you needed to hike to a mountain or trying to travel to North Germany, Swiss or the Arctics. you need different shell then what I'm recommended here. 
	- 
	- Shell 1: a rain jacket from any brands (Columbia, The North Faces, Pantagonia, ...)
		- How it works: it's most likely a synthetic, light coat with a plastic layer. The plastic pores is small enough for water droplets to not enter inside while the vapor could get out when it's dry and not raining. So it waterproof in the rain and breathable when dry. 
		- Advantages:
			  - It's very light, small, portable 
			  - More fashionable than a poncho raincoat. You will not wear a poncho everywhere but you can wear this shell. 
			  - Windproof and waterproof functional add on whenever you need
			  - Easy to clean. Remember to not tumble dry it.
			  - Could be use in other season
		- Disadvantage
			- It will not warm you up.
	- Shell 2: A down shell:
		- How it works: It's a 3 layer system: outer layer from synthetic for windproof and water resist, middle layer is fabric made from duck down + feather and inside keep you warm by a layer of fleece. The middle layer is the secret, the puffiness traps air inside, and it's not a big balloon of air, it's thousand of micro spaces that help keep the air warm, it's basically an insulated layers, the heat inside is trap while the cold outside is repelled for it has to freeze the trapped air layer first. 
		- Advantages:
			- Down is the best at keeping warm while it's very light
			- Someone think that puffiness is fashionable
			- Water resistant, windproof + insulate
			- The greatest ally when tackle unknown weather
		
		- Disadvantages:
			- Down work best when it's dry, when it got wet, the air pockets collapse and it became a wet expensive garbage bag. It's water resist but it will go down if it rain or snow a lot. 
			- Down required a long time to dry, it's expensive so you won't dare to tumble dry it, the material also require you to hit it after dried or taken out of vacuum to recover the air pockets.
			- When it rain suddenly, protect the down with rain coat or poncho, keep it dry. But also understand that doing so will reduce the breathability to nil. You will be steam in your setup, In case of short rain, light snow, it's better to trust the down water resistant. 
	- Shell 3: Fleece jacket
		- Fleeces is synthetic wool.
		- How it works: It's basically mimic wool with plastic fibers, 
			-
		- Advantages:
			- Cheap, you can buy a fleeces in Uniqlo at 10 eu
			- Warm, it's basically wool, as long as you don't let it touch you skin, it's warm, breathable.
			- Water resist, wind resist
				- It could be windproof depend on the production process
				- It could be treated with plastic to be waterproof or almost waterproof
			- Fashionable, much much prettier than the raincoat or down's waterproof plastic look, the fine, satin like finish of fleeces make it the best looking shell
			- Very easy to maintain, machine wash, tumble dry, hang dry, etc. 
		- Disadvantage:
			- It is not a durable material, it's made from plastic. Every times you wash it, the plastic shed, it will lose the shine after a few years of constant washing. The cheapness also add to the waste problem.
			- It's heavy and dense. It will cost a lot of space in any luggage so 2 fleeces pieces might be the limit.
		- Blend of wool and synthetic are also very common, but I won't say that it's best in both world. The waste problem is still presence, the feeling won't be premium like 100% wool. You also cannot keep the shine of fleeces with high content of wools while at low percent of wool, you are just paying more for a synthetic fleeces.

	- Shell 4: Wool 
		- Now that I reread this section after 2 days, wool might need its own section
		- Hard wool, tightly knitted wool sweater. They often are called fishing sweater
		- How it works: 
			- The fabric is tough, it was knitted tightly that proofing both water and wind
		- Advantages:
			- Good ones last a lifetime
			- It's stylish in a very vintage, timeless way
			
		- Disadvantages
			- Wool shells generally are not easy to taken off. That would limit the layering capability but you can have a very minimal Base + Shell with them. 
			- Very hard to have perfect fit.
			- It's expensive, expecting at least 100 eu/one (+shipping fee). Vintage (second hand market) could be found on ebay starting from 50 eu.
			- It's hard to buy, it felt more like a souvenir than a tools.
			- Need to learn about the material.
			- Can it be use in Vietnam ?

			- You paid not only for the material but also compensation for the workers
